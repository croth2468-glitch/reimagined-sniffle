Retatrutide (“Reta”): What the Published Evidence Shows

Real Health Tide · Research brief · Updated October 2026

Retatrutide (LY3437943) is an investigational once-weekly peptide from Eli Lilly that agonizes three receptors: GIP, GLP-1 and glucagon. This brief summarizes the mechanism, the phase 2 data, the reported safety signals and where the phase 3 program stands, with links to primary sources.

Key points
  • Triple agonist: GIP + GLP-1 + glucagon receptors. Semaglutide targets one, tirzepatide two.
  • Phase 2 (NEJM, 2023): up to 24.2% mean weight loss at 48 weeks at the highest dose.
  • Not approved by the FDA, EMA or any major regulator. Evidence comes from sponsor-run trials.
  • Material sold online as “retatrutide” is outside these trials and its identity and purity are unverified.

Mechanism

GLP-1 receptor activity reduces appetite and slows gastric emptying. GIP receptor activity is thought to add to insulin response and may help tolerability. Glucagon receptor activity is the distinguishing element: it is hypothesized to raise energy expenditure and lower hepatic fat, which is why retatrutide is being studied beyond weight loss, including in fatty liver disease. Phase 1 work reported a half-life of roughly six days, which supports weekly dosing.

Phase 2 obesity results

In the 48-week phase 2 trial in adults with obesity (Jastreboff et al., N Engl J Med 2023), mean change in body weight was dose-dependent:

ArmMean weight change, week 48
Placebo−2.1%
1 mg−8.7%
4 mg−17.1%
8 mg−22.8%
12 mg−24.2%

Weight had not plateaued at 48 weeks in the higher-dose arms. Separate phase 2 data in type 2 diabetes reported improvements in glycemic control alongside weight reduction.

Safety signals

  • Gastrointestinal: nausea, diarrhea and vomiting were the most common events and were more frequent at higher doses and with faster dose escalation.
  • Heart rate: dose-related increases were reported, peaking around 24 weeks and then declining.
  • Dysesthesia: abnormal skin sensations were reported more often at higher doses than with placebo.
  • Discontinuation: discontinuation due to adverse events rose with dose.

Long-term safety, durability after stopping, lean-mass effects and cardiovascular outcomes remain open questions pending phase 3.
