Semaglutide: An FDA-Approved GLP-1 Medicine, Explained

Real Health Tide · Research brief · Updated October 2026

Semaglutide is a once-weekly GLP-1 receptor agonist made by Novo Nordisk. It is sold as Ozempic and Rybelsus (type 2 diabetes) and Wegovy (chronic weight management and cardiovascular risk reduction). This brief covers how it works, what the large trials found and what to know about safety.

Key points
  • Approved by the FDA: Ozempic (2017), Wegovy (2021). Available by prescription only.
  • STEP 1: average weight loss of 14.9% at 68 weeks on 2.4 mg, versus 2.4% on placebo.
  • SELECT: 20% fewer major cardiovascular events in adults with obesity and existing heart disease.
  • Common side effects are gastrointestinal. A prescriber decides whether it is appropriate.

How it works

Semaglutide mimics GLP-1, a gut hormone released after eating. It acts on receptors in the brain to reduce appetite, slows stomach emptying, and helps the pancreas release insulin when blood sugar is high. Its half-life is about one week, which is why it is dosed weekly.

Safety

  • Common: nausea, vomiting, diarrhea and constipation, usually worst during dose increases.
  • Boxed warning: risk of thyroid C-cell tumors seen in rodents. Not for people with a personal or family history of medullary thyroid carcinoma or MEN 2.
  • Other warnings: pancreatitis, gallbladder disease, low blood sugar with insulin or sulfonylureas, and kidney problems from dehydration.

Anyone considering semaglutide should talk to a licensed clinician. Be cautious of non-prescription or compounded products sold online, whose quality is not guaranteed.
